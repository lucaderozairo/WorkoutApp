# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Performance-focused athletes — competitive runners, cyclists, and strength athletes who train with structure and periodization. They use this app before, during, and after sessions: reviewing readiness scores before deciding how hard to push, logging workouts precisely, and analyzing trends over weeks and training blocks. They are comfortable with data density and expect the app to behave like an instrument, not a motivational tool.

## Product Purpose

A personal fitness tracking app that turns training data into actionable insight. Dashboard aggregates readiness, conditions, and upcoming schedule. Log captures workouts and cardio sessions. Progress and Analytics surface trends and patterns. Social handles group training and scheduling. Profile owns readiness and athlete configuration. Success looks like: the athlete makes better training decisions because the data is instantly legible.

## Brand Personality

Precise, clinical, trustworthy. The voice of a sports scientist, not a personal trainer. Information should feel measured and credible — never hype, never cheerleading. The app earns trust by being consistent, accurate, and undecorated.

## Anti-references

- **Any lifestyle aesthetic where the design upstages the data**: If the user notices the design more than the numbers, something went wrong.

## Design Principles

1. **Data is the hero.** Every visual element exists to carry information or create space for it. Decoration that doesn't serve legibility is a defect.
2. **Precision over personality.** The app should feel like a performance instrument — a heart-rate monitor, not a poster. Confident and quiet.
3. **Respect complexity.** Performance athletes track many variables simultaneously. Honor that density; don't simplify away what they need.
4. **Trust through consistency.** Identical patterns for identical situations. When the athlete sees a pill, a chart, or a stat tile, it behaves exactly as it did before. Surprise erodes trust.
5. **Editorial restraint.** Visual storytelling, photography, and richer presentation are allowed when they clarify context or strengthen the athlete's narrative. They should support the training data, not compete with it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Practical implications: ≥4.5:1 contrast for body text, ≥3:1 for large text, full keyboard navigation, screen reader labels on all interactive controls and data visualizations, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected throughout. High-contrast mode already implemented via `[data-contrast="high"]`.
